Due to the 375m vs 1km spatial resolution difference (~7x area difference per pixel at nadir), VIIRS detects between 3.0x and 4.5x more active fire pixels over the same perimeter.
MODIS (Terra & Aqua) Baseline Heritage
  • Nominal Resolution: 1,000 meters (1.0 km nadir)
  • Pixel Area Growth (Bowtie Distortion): 1.0 km² → ~9.6 km² at edge
  • Equatorial Overpass (Local Solar): Terra: 10:30 AM/PM | Aqua: 13:30 PM/AM
  • Confidence Representation: Percentage [0 - 100%] Bayesian probability
  • Historical Continuity: Continuous archive since 2000 (Terra) / 2002 (Aqua)
VIIRS (NOAA-20, NOAA-21, Suomi-NPP) High-Resolution Continuity
  • Nominal Resolution: 375 meters (I-band)
  • Pixel Area Growth (3:1 Aggregation): 0.14 km² → ≤ 0.6 km² at edge
  • Equatorial Overpass (Local Solar): 13:30 PM/AM (50-min phased orbit)
  • Confidence Representation: Categorical ('low', 'nominal', 'high')
  • Operational Status: NOAA-20 & NOAA-21 operational continuity
Relative Fire Activity Index (RFAI) Dimensionless Harmonization
RFAI normalizes each sensor against its independent historical seasonal median ($RFAI = Count / Median_{month}$). This enables scientifically valid trend comparison without inventing artificial calibration constants.
FRP Distribution (Fire Radiative Power) Megawatts (MW)
Comparison of pixel-level radiative power quantiles. MODIS 1km pixels aggregate more thermal output per pixel, while VIIRS resolves smaller sub-pixel burning components.

🛰️ About PyroNexa & The NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 Challenge Solution

PyroNexa is a state-of-the-art satellite fire intelligence platform built for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge under the challenge category "Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots".

Earth observation satellites provide indispensable situational awareness for wildfire monitoring, forest conservation, and disaster response. However, combining NASA's two flagship thermal observation sensors—MODIS (onboard Terra and Aqua) and VIIRS (onboard Suomi NPP, NOAA-20, and NOAA-21)—presents severe scientific challenges due to substantial discrepancies in spatial resolution, scan geometry, detection thresholds, and confidence representations.

PyroNexa solves this harmonization dilemma through a transparent, mathematically rigorous framework that preserves scientific provenance without fabricating false equivalences or uncalibrated regression coefficients.

Scientific Architecture & Harmonization Principles
1. Discrete Spatial Grid Binning
Hotspots are aggregated into standardized 0.25° (~25 km) grid cells, tracking both raw detection density and unique burning cell area.
2. Ground Footprint Area (FPA) Normalization
Accounts for MODIS panoramic bowtie expansion (~1.0 km² to ~9.6 km²) versus VIIRS 3:1 along-scan sample aggregation (≤ 0.6 km²).
3. Relative Fire Activity Index (RFAI)
Evaluates observations against independent sensor historical monthly medians, yielding a dimensionless comparative index ($RFAI = Count / Median_{month}$).
4. Robust Dual Anomaly Detection
Calculates both parametric Z-scores and Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) on calendar-month subsets to filter out seasonal fire cycle confounding.

NASA FIRMS API Key Setup Free Access

To connect PyroNexa to live NASA FIRMS satellite telemetry:

  1. Request a free MAP key from NASA FIRMS.
  2. Add your key to the project root .env file: FIRMS_MAP_KEY=your_key_here.
  3. Restart the server or click the API Key button in the header.
Note: Without a key, PyroNexa automatically runs in Simulation Mode using multi-year verified NASA sample datasets.
